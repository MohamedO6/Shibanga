A changing landscape, close to home

Land can sink, volcanoes can swell, and fields can change through a season. Radar reveals changes that are difficult to notice from the ground. Reading its products, however, usually requires specialist tools and knowledge.

We are Team Shiblanga from Egypt. We chose the Nile Delta as our starting point: a landscape where farms, cities and the Mediterranean meet. We built Undula so that someone can start with a place they recognise, explore what is changing, and inspect the evidence behind the explanation.

Start with a place. Open its story, inspect its observations, and explore the physical processes that could explain them. Globe labels provide published context and model examples, distinct from the NISAR observations.

Make the radar explorable

Select a location and play its radar sequence. Move between dated observations, compare Then & Now optical images, or search NASA's catalogue for the latest available NISAR GCOV preview at another location. Catalogue coverage determines what is available.

Undula covers ten locations affected by subsidence, volcanic activity or faults. The bundle includes 93 unique GUNW interferogram pairs across nine locations, with 17 pairs processed from full products: nine in Central Valley and eight in the Nile Delta. The other pairs are browse previews; the tenth location provides context without a bundled NISAR sequence.

Test an interpretation with another satellite

Our case study asks: is the seasonal change in radar backscatter consistent with vegetation greenness measured independently?

We measured HH and HV gamma0 backscatter from ten full NISAR GCOV dates, June–October 2026, across a 20 × 20 km area near Kafr El Sheikh, on track 129_A_017. HV rose by about 3.1 dB to an August peak, then declined. Seasonal vegetation change is a possible interpretation of that pattern.

We tested it against Landsat 8/9 surface-reflectance NDVI. Fixed date and quality rules accepted eight matches within four calendar days; two radar dates had no acceptable optical match. Quality masks exclude cloud and other contaminated pixels. Every accepted optical date uses the same valid area, covering 98.3% of the footprint.

The primary HV/NDVI Spearman correlation is 0.67. The secondary HH correlation is 0.76; we report it too, even though HV was our primary question. The first-difference rate check is 0.86 across seven intervals, while leaving one date out gives HV correlations from 0.54 to 0.82.

The observations support a seasonal interpretation at regional scale. These correlations are descriptive associations, not accuracy percentages. One area, one season and eight matched observations do not establish crop identity, flooding, causation or performance elsewhere. The shaded interval describes spatial resampling variability and omits sensor and atmospheric uncertainty.

We publish the methods and sensitivity checks, paired values, and scene IDs and exclusions.

Give motion another language

In model mode, each whole centimetre of modelled relative line-of-sight motion triggers a note. Notes descend for subsidence, rise for uplift, and alternate in stereo for sideways motion. In radar mode, a short ping marks progression between interferogram pairs.

Listen to an eight-second example of the designed descending notes.

This excerpt illustrates model sonification. It is not an Earth sound recording or an audio track synchronized with the GIFs. In the application, users can turn sound on and move the model's time slider themselves.

The Interference Lab lets users change a physical model and inspect the resulting interferogram. Together, sound and direct manipulation provide additional ways to explore the relationship between motion and signal.

Ask what land motion changes

Sea 2100 compares the Delta with and without continued subsidence under fixed-rate assumptions. The simplified elevation-threshold scenario shows approximately 3,491 km² versus 1,557 km² of additional area below the modelled sea threshold by 2100.

The full scenario grows from 2026 to 2100, retaining today's baseline water while additional threshold exposure starts at zero. It illustrates continued fixed rates, while omitting a full simulation of defences, pumps, tides, hydraulic connectivity and changing rates. It is not a forecast of actual inundation.

Keep the evidence inspectable

We distinguish measured observations, derived results and model outputs. GUNW processing masks invalid pixels and converts corrected phase to relative line-of-sight estimates. In the Delta cumulative sequence, about 4.7% of the displayed grid remains valid; other pixels are unknown. No usable simultaneous Delta GNSS comparison was obtained, and the Central Valley comparison did not resolve small motion against a zero-motion baseline. Landsat tests the seasonal interpretation, not displacement accuracy.

Source links, processing scripts and an asset audit make the work inspectable. Time-stamped evidence records help check record integrity; scientific validity still depends on the observations and methods.

Who it can help

Undula is designed for students, educators and residents who want to explore changing landscapes without installing radar-processing software. Its current contribution is a working educational prototype and a reproducible regional case study. We have not yet measured learning outcomes or demonstrated operational use by farmers or authorities.

Next, we plan structured user testing, comparisons across additional seasons and locations, and stronger independent observations before making more specific environmental claims.

The app is open source under the MIT licence. It uses JavaScript, HTML, CSS, WebGL, Leaflet and Web Audio, with Python tools for data processing and review. Explore the code and documentation.

Claude and OpenAI Codex assisted code, analysis scripts and writing under team direction and review. Google Flow was used for the astronaut characters, and OpenAI imagegen for the Journey illustrations. Satellite and GNSS observations come from cited sources; AI was not used to fabricate those observations.
